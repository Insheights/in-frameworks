# IIVCS Adoption Sequencing & Maturity Model

**Where to start · What sequence to follow · What early, developing, and mature adoption looks like across the suite**

---

This model answers the two questions every organization asks before adopting the [Insheights Integrated Value Creation System (IIVCS)](README.md):

1. **Where do we start** — [Organization Strategy Model (OSM)](README.md#osm--organization-strategy-model), [Product Transformation System (PTS)](README.md#pts--product-transformation-system), or [Customer Value Delivery Model (CVDM)](README.md#cvdm--customer-value-delivery-model) — and in what sequence?
2. **How mature are we** — what does early, developing, and mature adoption look like, and what would it take to advance?

It is a self-orientation tool. For a directional reading use the [IIVCS Quick Self-Assessment](quick-assessment.md); for a structured, scored evaluation use the IIVCS Detailed Assessment. This model is the map that makes either result actionable: it tells you where you are and what to do next.

> **The core idea:** IIVCS maturity is not the average of how good each framework is in isolation. It is how well the frameworks are **connected as a system** — strategy governing an operating model that realizes value at the customer journey, with signal flowing back upstream. An organization can run a strong operating model and still be *early* as a system if nothing connects it to strategy or customer reality.
>
> Maturity here means **adoption fidelity** — the frameworks present and connected — *not* proof of value created. It is a precondition for value, not evidence of it: a mature reading says the machinery is in place and wired together, not that the organization is yet creating more value.

---

## The Three Maturity Levels

Adoption maturity is defined at three levels across the suite. The levels describe the **system**, not any single framework.

| Level | What it looks like across the suite |
|---|---|
| **Early** | One or two components operate in isolation. Measurement is activity- or output-based. There is no upstream feedback loop — signals die at the team. Frameworks, if present, are not connected to each other. |
| **Developing** | Multiple components are connected on at least one value stream. [Efficiency, Experience, Effectiveness (3E)](3e.md) is applied in places; one [Adoption, Engagement, Retention (AER)](aer.md) track (usually customer) is measured. Some feedback loops are active and reach PTS. Strategy, operating model, and delivery are partially linked but not yet a closed system. |
| **Mature** | All three frameworks operate as one connected system on the value stream. Dual-track AER (Customer + Organizational) is measured in parallel; the 8 Values aggregate from their operational source; the full upstream feedback loop (CVDM → PTS → OSM) revises strategy on signal. The system is self-correcting. |

## Maturity Across the Suite

Read each row and find the column you have fully reached today. Four rows form the chain strategy → operating model → delivery → feedback: **OSM, PTS, CVDM and the Feedback System**. Your **system level is the lowest of those four rows**, not their average: the IIVCS is a chain, and a chain is only as strong as its weakest link. Three rows, **3E, AER and Product Mindset**, are **enablers**: the measurement and the working culture the three frameworks depend on. The **8 Values** row is the **outcome reading**: whether you can see the value your chain produces, read from whether value is measured, traced and reviewed, not how much value you create. Enablers and the outcome reading are read alongside the chain rather than counted as links. An enabler below your system level is a finding: it shows a framework being run without the measurement or ways of working it needs, and it limits how far that framework can climb. An outcome reading below your system level means you cannot yet see the value your chain produces; it is expected while the chain is young. Where the chain breaks tells you where to start (see [From an assessment result](#from-an-assessment-result)).

| Component | Early | Developing | Mature |
|---|---|---|---|
| **OSM** — Strategy & Governance | Strategy is a deck; priorities are activities, not outcomes; no stated diagnosis or choices; no value-stream governance | Value priorities stated as outcomes and mapped to some value streams; diagnosis, choices, and hypotheses are partly implicit; governance cadence exists but rarely revises priorities | Priorities rest on an explicit diagnosis and choices (where to play and not play, how to win), carry written hypotheses, are funded in rank order, and are governed by value stream; Governance & Measurement reconciles dual-track AER + 8 Values, tests the hypotheses, and revises strategy on signal |
| **PTS** — Operating Model | Project-based delivery; functional silos and handoffs; teams disband per initiative | Some persistent cross-functional teams on key value streams, able to deliver without routine handoffs; partial [Product Operating Model (POM)](glossary.md#core-system-and-frameworks) Enabler activation; the teams' uptake of the way of working (practice coverage) read from their own records against a baseline | Persistent value-stream teams; all four POM Enablers active; continuous lifecycle; Organizational AER measured from team records: adoption, engagement, and retention of the way of working |
| **CVDM** — Delivery & Realization | Delivery organized by internal process, not the customer journey; no journey-to-value-stream map | Journey mapped to value stream on key journeys; value stages defined with a stated customer-outcome promise; at least two feedback loops active | Full journey-to-value-stream mapping; value stages defined as outcomes; all five loops active, those that read the customer on the customer's own words or behavior; Customer AER measured |
| **3E** — Operational Measurement | Metrics are activity/output; no consistent measurement language | 3E applied at some elements; Use → Feel → Achieve read in places | 3E applied consistently across architecture levels and read in sequence diagnostically |
| **AER** — Behavioral Measurement | No behavioral measurement; satisfaction surveys at best | One track (usually Customer AER) defined, baselined and reviewed on a cadence on at least one named primary journey | Dual-track AER (Customer + Organizational) measured in parallel and reconciled at OSM |
| **8 Values** — Value Measurement | Value measured only financially/operationally, in silos | Several value dimensions tracked, each with a baseline and a current reading, and reviewed; some traceable to an operational source | All 8 Values aggregated from their operational source and feeding OSM governance |
| **Feedback System** — Upstream Loops | No upstream feedback; signals die at the team | Operational loops active, each naming who acts on it and the decision it informs, and reaching PTS; feedback has changed how delivery is organized | Full upstream path CVDM → PTS → OSM; operational + organizational feedback drives strategic reassessment |
| **Product Mindset** — Foundation | Output-focused, project culture; assumptions go untested | Outcome language adopted; discovery practiced in places; some initiatives framed as testable hypotheses | Customer obsession, outcome-over-output, continuous discovery, hypothesis-driven thinking, and cross-functional ownership are the norm |

> **How to read your result:** Note the column for each row. A row sits at a level only when it has *fully* reached it — the scored Detailed Assessment makes this explicit by *gating* (you earn a level by meeting all its criteria, not by averaging close to it). Scattered results (some Mature, some Early) are the most common and the most useful finding — they show exactly which connection is missing. Your break, the weakest link on the chain, is your next adoption priority.

**How the Product Mindset principles take hold.** They grow through the work, not through announcements.

- **Early → Developing:** start with one team and a few principles: frame the pilot team's goals as outcomes, find out what customers need on one piece of work before building it, and frame one piece of work as a hypothesis to test. Explain why, and give the team time to learn how.
- **Developing → Mature:** make all five the team's routine: goal reviews judge progress against outcomes, every significant piece of work starts with discovery and a stated hypothesis, and decisions start from customer evidence. Leaders ask for that evidence in their own reviews.
- **Holding Mature:** new team members learn the principles in the work itself, and leaders keep asking for the evidence. When they stop, the principles are expected to fade first.

## Self-Assessment

Two assessment artifacts place you on this model. Use whichever fits the moment:

- **[IIVCS Quick Self-Assessment](quick-assessment.md)** — a directional reading in a few minutes: one question per IIVCS element, each answer mapping to Early / Developing / Mature. Start here.
- **IIVCS Detailed Assessment** — the rigorous, scored evaluation: evidence-anchored criteria across all eight components, gated into a full maturity profile with strengths, constraint, and adoption priority. Maintained privately by Insheights.

Both ask the same thing the grid above does — *where is each element, and where does the chain break?* — and both point back here for what to do next.

> **Assess one value stream at a time.** IIVCS maturity is about how the frameworks connect to deliver value — and they connect *on a value stream*. Assess a single, named value stream end-to-end, not the whole organization at once.

**Describe the organization by its streams.** A level belongs to a value stream. To describe the whole organization, say how many of its streams sit at each level, for example "one stream Mature, two Developing, three not yet assessed". Never average them into one organization-wide level.

### Assessment is periodic, not one-time

Maturity is a moving state, not a milestone. Assess on a recurring **cadence** — re-run the assessment against the same value stream at the rhythm of your strategic governance review (typically quarterly), so movement is visible and the result keeps driving action. A one-time score tells you where you were; a repeated one tells you whether the system is actually becoming more connected.

- **Same value stream, same questions, regular interval** — so each reading is comparable to the last.
- **Watch your *system level* move, not the average** — a rising level means the frameworks are connecting; a flat level with a rising average means components are improving in isolation.
- **Re-run out of cycle** after any significant change — a strategy reset, an operating-model change, a major delivery shift, or a change of sponsor or value-stream owner can move maturity up or down, and the assessment should confirm which.
- **When one value stream reaches Mature, assess the next** — maturity spreads value stream by value stream, not in a single organization-wide leap.

### Holding a level

A level is **reached** when a scheduled reading first meets it, and **held** once it is met at three scheduled readings in a row: two show it is stable, three show a trend. One reading below a held level is a prompt to look for the signs below. Two readings below it in a row mean the level has **slipped**, and it is no longer held. An out-of-cycle re-run can show a slip but does not count toward held. These counts are a starting rule, to be calibrated.

A level holds while the people and routines that earned it stay in place: the sponsor stays, governance keeps reading the stream, the teams stay persistent, and the stream's feedback loops keep informing decisions. **Signs of backsliding** to look for at each reading:

- the sponsor or the value-stream owner changes;
- active feedback loops slip back to planned, or a loop misses its cadence twice;
- teams are re-formed per project, or funding moves back to projects;
- Organizational AER falls, especially practice persistence.

## Before You Start

An assessment needs no preparation. Adopting IIVCS does: before a pilot starts, name two people and what they can decide.

- **An executive sponsor**, who can protect the pilot team's time and stop work that competes with it.
- **A value-stream owner** for the pilot stream, who decides trade-offs inside it.

Their roles are set out in the [OSM](guides/osm-practitioners-guide.md) and [PTS](guides/pts-practitioners-guide.md) guides. Without a named sponsor, run the assessment and use the result to find one; do not start the pilot.

## Adoption Sequencing — Where to Start

The IIVCS is designed so **OSM governs, PTS operationalizes, CVDM realizes**, with feedback running upstream (CVDM → PTS → OSM). That gives a canonical top-down sequence — but you start where your pain is, then backfill. There are three valid entry points.

### Entry point 1 — Strategy-led (start with OSM)

**Start here when:** strategic intent is unclear, value priorities are contested, or execution is drifting from intent. The break is between strategy and everything below it.

**Sequence:** OSM → PTS → CVDM. Define value priorities and value-stream outcomes first, build the operating model to deliver them, then wire the customer journey to realize them.

### Entry point 2 — Operating-model-led (start with PTS)

**Start here when:** strategy is reasonably clear but delivery is broken — projects not products, handoffs, no value-stream ownership. The break is between intent and execution.

**Sequence:** PTS → CVDM → formalize OSM. Stand up persistent value-stream teams and the operating model around existing priorities, push into CVDM at the journey, then formalize OSM governance as the feedback loop matures.

### Entry point 3 — Delivery-led (start with CVDM)

**Start here when:** there is an acute, visible customer problem — a broken journey, failing retention — and you need a fast proof of value. The break is at the point of delivery.

**Sequence:** CVDM → PTS → OSM. Fix one customer journey end-to-end on a single value stream, then pull upstream — using CVDM's feedback loops to inform the operating model (PTS) and strategy (OSM).

| If your situation is… | Start with | Then |
|---|---|---|
| Strategy unclear / execution drifting from intent | **OSM** | PTS → CVDM |
| Strategy clear, delivery broken (projects, handoffs) | **PTS** | CVDM → formalize OSM |
| Acute customer / journey / retention problem | **CVDM** | PTS → OSM |

### From an assessment result

Your **break** is the lowest of the four chain rows: OSM, PTS, CVDM and the Feedback System. If two or three are equally low, take the one furthest upstream. If all four are Early, follow the [recommended default path](#sequencing-principles) on one value stream. The break gives your default entry point:

| Break at | Default entry point |
|---|---|
| OSM | **Entry point 1** — strategy-led (OSM) |
| PTS | **Entry point 2** — operating-model-led (PTS) |
| CVDM | **Entry point 3** — delivery-led (CVDM) |
| The Feedback System | Whichever of OSM, PTS and CVDM is lowest (the furthest upstream on a tie): feedback is repaired at the framework that feeds it |

This is a default, not a rule: start where your pain is. If your situation, in the table above, points to another entry point, start there and note why.

**No break.** If all four chain rows are Mature, the chain has no break. An enabler or the outcome reading (the 8 Values row) below Mature is then your next priority, the lowest first; with none, keep re-assessing on your cadence.

## Sequencing Principles

Whatever the entry point, four principles govern a healthy adoption:

- **Define before operationalize before realize.** Outcomes should be defined (OSM) before the operating model is built (PTS) before delivery is wired (CVDM). Starting downstream is valid — but you will have to backfill the upstream definition, or the work has no anchor.
- **Start narrow.** Adopt on a *single value stream*, end-to-end, before scaling across the suite. One connected value stream proves the system; ten disconnected pilots prove nothing.
- **Measurement follows function.** Apply 3E as soon as one architecture element exists; add AER once delivery and the operating model run; aggregate the 8 Values once the system produces outcomes to read. Do not stand up a board scorecard before the underlying signals exist.
- **Feedback matures last.** The full upstream loop (CVDM → PTS → OSM) is the final thing to reach maturity, because it requires all three frameworks operating and connected. It is the signature of a mature system, not a starting condition.

> **Recommended default path:** Pilot on one value stream — define its outcome (OSM), stand up a persistent team and operating model for it (PTS), wire its customer journey (CVDM) — then activate the feedback loops and only then scale to the next value stream. Top-down to establish the anchor; bottom-up feedback to keep it honest. What a pilot needs, its first visible win, and when to widen are set out in the [PTS guide](guides/pts-practitioners-guide.md#before-you-start).

## Connection to the Assessments

This model is the qualitative map; the assessments place an organization on it. The IIVCS Detailed Assessment spans all IIVCS components — OSM, PTS, CVDM, 3E, AER, 8 Values, the Feedback System, and Product Mindset — and produces a maturity profile identifying strengths, gaps, and adoption priorities.

The pieces work together:

- The **[Quick Self-Assessment](quick-assessment.md)** and the **Detailed Assessment** tell you *where you are* — directionally, or as a scored profile across the components above.
- This **model** tells you *what to do next* — which level you are at, which connection is your constraint, and where to start.

---

## Summary

- IIVCS maturity is measured by **connection**, not by any single framework's strength in isolation.
- The three levels — **Early, Developing, Mature** — describe the system: from isolated components, to partial connection on a value stream, to a self-correcting system with a closed upstream feedback loop.
- There are **three valid entry points** — strategy-led (OSM), operating-model-led (PTS), or delivery-led (CVDM) — chosen by where the break is, then backfilled toward a connected system.
- Adopt **one value stream end-to-end first**, let measurement follow function, and treat the full upstream feedback loop as the signature of maturity.
- **Assess on a cadence, not once.** Re-run the assessment against the same value stream at your governance rhythm — maturity is a moving state, and the point is to watch your system level move over time.

See [Framework Positioning](positioning.md) for how the three frameworks relate, and the [Feedback System](feedback.md) for the upstream loop that defines mature adoption.
