# Product Transformation System (PTS) — Practitioners Guide

**How to stand up a Product Operating Model around value streams, empower persistent cross-functional teams, and run the continuous product lifecycle that carries strategy into delivery.**

---

## At a Glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **What it does** | Structures the value-stream operating model and empowers the persistent cross-functional teams that turn [Organization Strategy Model (OSM)](../README.md#osm--organization-strategy-model)'s strategic priorities into delivered value through [Customer Value Delivery Model (CVDM)](../README.md#cvdm--customer-value-delivery-model) |
| **When to use it** | Operationalizing strategy; moving from project-to-product; when teams deliver output but not outcomes; when handoffs, not value streams, organize the work |
| **Who runs it** | Transformation/product leadership; value-stream owners; the leaders accountable for the operating model |
| **Who must be in the room** | Executive sponsor (mandate), value-stream owners, each Core team's accountable product role and cross-functional team leads (product, engineering, design, ops), HR/org-design (team topology), change & comms lead (Launch) |
| **Key inputs** | OSM's governed value-stream outcomes and priorities; value-stream inventory; current org/team structure; Product Mindset maturity |
| **Key outputs** | An activated Product Operating Model (4 POM Enablers); cross-functional teams structured around value streams; a running product lifecycle; Organizational [Adoption, Engagement, Retention (AER)](../aer.md) measurement |
| **Typical timeframe** | Initial stand-up: a pilot on one value stream, for the duration it needs to run; widening thereafter. Lifecycle: continuous and overlapping |

> **New to the [Insheights Integrated Value Creation System (IIVCS)](../README.md)?** This guide uses system terms — POM and the POM Enablers, value stream, value-stream outcome, [Efficiency, Experience, Effectiveness (3E)](../3e.md), AER, the 8 Values, OSM, CVDM, Product Mindset — defined in the [Glossary](../glossary.md). [Framework Positioning](../positioning.md) explains how PTS relates to the other frameworks in a few minutes; read it first if you are starting here.

> **Not a product organization?** "Product team" → "delivery unit / care team / service team"; "customer" → "patient / citizen / client"; "product lifecycle" → "service delivery lifecycle." The operating-model logic is unchanged — only the vocabulary shifts. See [Translation Notes](#translation-notes).

> **Where this fits:** PTS is the operating-model layer of the IIVCS — it **operationalizes** OSM's governed priorities into the system that delivers them, and **enables** CVDM by supplying the empowered teams and capabilities that realize value. It returns the *executability* signal up to OSM and receives operational feedback from CVDM. See [Framework Positioning](../positioning.md).

## Before You Start

**Inputs required**

- [ ] **Governed value-stream outcomes from OSM** — the business intent each value stream exists to produce, with owners. PTS builds the operating model to deliver these; without them, you are restructuring teams with no outcome to anchor on.
- [ ] **Value-stream inventory and capability map from OSM** — the value streams teams are organized around (not functions), any new or reshaped stream OSM has chartered for PTS to build, and the organization-wide [capability map](../glossary.md#architecture-elements) PTS records ownership on.
- [ ] **Current org and team structure** — how people, reporting lines, and capabilities are arranged today, so you can see the gap to a value-stream topology.
- [ ] **Product Mindset maturity reading** — the organization's readiness to operate with customer obsession, outcome focus, continuous discovery, hypothesis-driven thinking, and cross-functional ownership.
- [ ] **The organization's funding process, role titles, and forums** — PTS works through them rather than installing new ones; see *What PTS asks of your existing structures* below.
- [ ] **A change & communication capacity** — PTS changes how people work; the Launch phase and adoption depend on a real change function, not an announcement.

**People required**

| Role | Why they must be present |
|---|---|
| Executive sponsor | Holds the mandate to restructure teams around value streams and protect persistent ownership |
| Value-stream owner(s) | Accountable for the value-stream outcome the operating model must deliver; decides trade-offs inside the stream within OSM's allocation |
| Accountable product role | One per Core team, whatever its title: accountable for the team's product and its priorities |
| Cross-functional team leads | Product, engineering, design, operations — they will own end-to-end delivery without handoffs |
| Org-design / HR partner | Designs the team topology and the persistent (not project) staffing model |
| Change & communications lead | Owns the internal adoption half of Launch — without this, the operating model is adopted on paper only |

**Prepare the room**

- Bring the OSM value-stream outcomes in writing — each with its owner and success definition. The operating model is built to deliver *these*.
- Map the current state honestly: where handoffs, queues, and functional silos sit today, and which value streams have no clear owner.
- Confirm the mandate to make persistent, not temporary, teams. PTS fails when "cross-functional teams" are reassembled project-by-project — persistence is the point.

**What PTS asks of your existing structures**

PTS uses the organization's own funding process, role titles and forums; it does not install new ones. Whatever the organization uses must come to satisfy three conditions as PTS runs; a pilot may start before the first is met:

- **Funding** goes to persistent value-stream teams, allocated to value-stream outcomes, not projects.
- **Each Core team has one named role accountable for its product**, whatever its title.
- **Trade-offs have one route.** The value-stream owner decides trade-offs inside the stream, within OSM's allocation; anything that moves funding or capacity between streams goes to OSM's decision forum.

Moving from project to product funding is part of the shift PTS supports, not a condition for starting.

**Start with a pilot**

Stand up one value stream first. At minimum, a pilot needs:

- the strategic objectives and value priorities it serves, set by OSM, or the organization's existing priorities where OSM is not yet in place;
- a persistent value-stream team;
- the value-stream outcomes it is accountable for;
- the duration it needs to run;
- the work the pilot team stops doing, named by the executive sponsor, so the pilot is not added on top of a full load.

A pilot's first visible win is its stream's first [active](../feedback.md#operational-feedback--cvdm) feedback loop: one that names who acts on it, the decision it informs and how its signal is collected, and has informed a decision. It is the first sign the system is working, before any level moves.

A pilot runs on the funding the organization has today. Before widening to the next stream, we recommend checking that the pilot's teams stayed together for its duration, its owned and consumed capabilities are on the organization-wide map, one Organizational AER read has been acted on, and one executability read has reached OSM's decision forum. The organization decides when and how to widen.

## How to Run It

Each step below is an action. Complete them in order — later steps depend on earlier outputs. Once stood up, the operating model and its lifecycle run continuously; Steps 1–3 are the stand-up, Steps 4–5 are the ongoing operation. Run the stand-up on one value stream first (see *Start with a pilot*).

---

### Step 1 — Receive governed priorities and confirm value-stream outcomes

**What you do:** Take OSM's governed value-stream outcomes and priorities. For each, confirm the value stream that will carry it, name its owner, and restate the value-stream outcome the operating model must produce. Return an early executability read to OSM: can this be operationalized with the teams and capabilities that exist?

**Key question this step answers:** *Which value-stream outcomes must this operating model deliver, and can we realistically carry them?*

**Inputs:** OSM governed value-stream outcomes; value-stream inventory and capability map; value-stream charters for new or reshaped streams
**Output:** Confirmed value-stream-to-outcome map with owners; a build plan for each chartered new or reshaped stream; an initial executability signal to OSM

**Guidance:**
- **Build what OSM charters.** When OSM creates or reshapes a value stream, its charter names the stakeholder, the outcome, the owner, and the build capacity committed under the priority that called for it. PTS plans and builds the stream's stages and teams within that capacity; once built, it runs as a standing value stream with persistent teams like any other. A build that cannot be carried within its committed capacity is an executability signal to OSM.
- PTS owns the *delivery* of the value-stream outcome; it does not redefine it. Customer outcomes (owned by product teams via CVDM) *evidence* the value-stream outcome — they do not constitute it. Hold that line.
- Surface executability honestly and early. A value-stream outcome OSM can articulate but PTS cannot carry is a measurable failure — and OSM needs that signal now, not after a year of trying.
- You are done when every priority maps to a value stream with a named owner and a restated outcome, and OSM has received the executability read.

---

### Step 2 — Stand up the Product Operating Model: activate the four POM Enablers

**What you do:** Build the operating model by activating each of the four POM Enablers — **Leadership**, **Practices & Behaviors**, **Organizational Architecture**, and **Culture & Relationships** — so they collectively empower teams to own and deliver value-stream outcomes.

**Key question this step answers:** *What operating model will empower teams to deliver these outcomes?*

**Inputs:** Value-stream-to-outcome map (Step 1); Product Mindset maturity reading
**Output:** An activated POM — each enabler defined, owned, and operating

**Guidance:**
- Activate all four — a partial POM under-delivers. The most common failure is restructuring teams (Organizational Architecture) while leaving Leadership setting feature lists, or Culture rewarding individual heroics over cross-functional ownership.
- The POM Enablers are how PTS builds **People Value** — the human system that delivers. Treat Leadership and Culture & Relationships as load-bearing, not soft.
- Anchor each enabler to a behavior change, not a document. "Practices & Behaviors" means teams actually run continuous discovery and hypothesis-driven delivery — not that a playbook exists.

**POM Enabler activation reference:**

| POM Enabler | What activating it means | Signal it is active |
|---|---|---|
| **Leadership** | Leaders set outcomes/[Objectives and Key Results (OKRs)](../glossary.md#external-frameworks-referenced), not feature lists; protect team autonomy and persistence | Teams are measured on value delivered, not tasks shipped |
| **Practices & Behaviors** | Continuous discovery, hypothesis-driven delivery, end-to-end ownership are the norm | Teams test assumptions before building; phases overlap continuously |
| **Organizational Architecture** | Persistent cross-functional teams aligned to value streams; minimized handoffs | Team boundaries match value streams, not functions |
| **Culture & Relationships** | Trust, psychological safety, and cross-functional collaboration are rewarded | Teams own problems together; failure surfaces as learning, not blame |

---

### Step 3 — Structure persistent cross-functional teams around value streams

**What you do:** Organize people into persistent cross-functional teams aligned to value streams — each team holding the product, engineering, design, and operational capability to own delivery end-to-end, without handoffs. Define each team's value-stream ownership and the customer outcomes it is accountable for through CVDM.

**Key question this step answers:** *Who owns each value stream end-to-end, and do they have what they need to deliver without handoffs?*

**Inputs:** Activated POM (Step 2); value-stream-to-outcome map; capability map (from OSM)
**Output:** A value-stream team topology — persistent teams by type, ownership boundaries, and for each team the capabilities it owns and the capabilities it consumes

**Guidance:**
- How existing team practices (SAFe, Scrum, Team Topologies) fit these team types is in [Integration with Existing Practices](../integration.md).
- Persistent, not project. Teams form around value streams and stay; they are not assembled and disbanded per initiative. Persistence is what lets ownership, learning, and customer knowledge compound.
- **Use three kinds of team, all aligned to value streams:**

  | Team | What it is | Aligned to |
  |---|---|---|
  | **Core** | A persistent cross-functional team aligned to one value stream. It owns the capabilities only that stream's stages use, consumes the rest, and is accountable for the stream's outcome end to end. | One value stream |
  | **Shared** | A persistent team that owns a capability value stages in several value streams draw on. It serves those streams' Core teams on agreed terms and is judged on their outcomes. | The value streams that draw on its capability |
  | **Platform-Enabled** | A persistent team that owns a technology capability, such as a platform, data and AI, integration or developer tooling, and offers it as a self-service internal product, so other teams consume it without hand-offs. | Every value stream that consumes it |

- **Record owned and consumed capabilities on the capability map.** Every capability has exactly one owning team; every other team that draws on it consumes it. A capability sourced from a partner is always consumed, with one internal team accountable for the partner, the integration, and the service level: the team that would own it if it were built in-house. A capability the map is missing, or a duplicate, goes back to OSM to decide; PTS does not add it locally.
- Each Core team must own or consume the capabilities to deliver end-to-end. Consuming a capability from a Shared or Platform-Enabled team, on agreed terms or as self-service, is not a handoff. If delivering an outcome needs an ad-hoc handoff to another team, either a capability is missing or the value-stream boundary is wrong — fix the topology, don't add a handoff.
- The team owns the **customer outcomes** that evidence the value-stream outcome; the value-stream owner owns the value-stream outcome itself. Make both explicit so accountability does not blur.
- You are done when every value stream has a persistent Core team with clear ownership and one named accountable product role, every capability on the map has one owning team, each team's owned and consumed capabilities are recorded, and no value stage depends on an ad-hoc hand-off.

---

### Step 4 — Run the continuous product lifecycle

**What you do:** Run the six continuous, overlapping phases — **Discover, Define, Develop, Deliver, Launch, Support** — underpinned by the feedback and learning loop. Teams move through them continuously per value stream, not as a one-time sequential project.

**Key question this step answers:** *How do teams continuously turn value-stream priorities into delivered, adopted customer value?*

**Inputs:** Value-stream team topology (Step 3); value-stream outcomes
**Output:** A running product lifecycle per value stream, with Launch covering both external go-to-market and internal change adoption

**Guidance:**
- How existing delivery practices (Scrum, SAFe, ITIL, Design Thinking) plug into the lifecycle is in [Integration with Existing Practices](../integration.md).
- The phases are continuous and overlapping, not a waterfall. Discover never stops — teams stay in contact with customer reality across every phase. Each phase is a testable hypothesis the feedback loop validates or corrects.
- **Launch has two halves, and both are required.** External: go-to-market — getting the value into customers' hands and driving adoption. Internal: change communication — getting the *organization* ready to operate, support, and sustain what was launched. A launch that nails GTM but neglects internal change produces customer demand the operating model cannot sustain.
- **Support is a continuous operating mode**, not an end-state handoff. It feeds delivery learning and discovery back into the loop — the value stream keeps running, it does not "finish."
- Watch for the lifecycle collapsing back into stage-gates with handoffs. If Discover only happens at the start and Support is a separate team, the operating model has reverted to projects.

**Product lifecycle phase reference:**

| Phase | What the team does | Continuous because |
|---|---|---|
| **Discover** | Learns from customers, data, and market signals; surfaces value opportunities | Customer reality never stops changing |
| **Define** | Frames the value hypothesis and the outcome to be delivered | New signal continuously reframes what matters |
| **Develop** | Builds the product/service capability to deliver the value | Built in small releases against tested hypotheses |
| **Deliver** | Puts the value into the value stream / customer journey (via CVDM) | Delivery is iterative, not a single release |
| **Launch** | External GTM + internal change communication and adoption | New value and audiences arrive continuously |
| **Support** | Sustains the delivered value; feeds learning back | Value must be sustained, not handed off and forgotten |

---

### Step 5 — Activate the feedback and learning loop

**What you do:** Stand up the continuous feedback and learning loop. Measure **Organizational AER** — whether teams adopt, engage with, and sustain the operating model. Return the executability signal upward to OSM; receive CVDM's operational feedback on whether the operating model performs at the delivery layer.

**Key question this step answers:** *Is the operating model being adopted and sustained — and is it actually delivering value-stream outcomes?*

**Inputs:** Running lifecycle (Step 4); CVDM operational feedback; OSM governance cadence
**Output:** Organizational AER measurement, the executability return path to OSM, and the operational feedback intake from CVDM

**Guidance:**
- PTS is the home of **Organizational AER** — the organizational half of the IIVCS dual-track measurement. Measure whether teams *adopt* the new way of working, *engage* with it, and *sustain* it. An operating model adopted on paper but not engaged with produces no value.
- Run both directions. Up: PTS returns executability and organizational readiness (People/Operational Value) to OSM's Governance & Measurement. Down: PTS receives CVDM's delivery-learning and performance-optimization feedback. PTS sits between them and must close both loops.
- Name who acts on the signal. Organizational AER that no leader acts on is a dashboard; the value-stream owner and POM Leadership must own retuning the operating model when adoption or engagement drops.
- Minimum cadence: continuous team-level learning; a value-stream operating-model review at the organization's planning cadence, held before each OSM decision forum so its read reaches it; executability roll-up to each OSM decision forum. Out-of-cycle trigger: a value-stream outcome the operating model cannot carry → executability escalation to OSM's decision forum, without waiting for the cadence.

### Example 1 — B2B fintech moving from project-to-product (operating-model stand-up)

> **Scenario:** A 600-person B2B payments company delivers through quarterly projects: a PMO assigns engineers to initiatives, hands specs to a separate delivery team, and disbands the team at launch. OSM has set a value-stream outcome — *merchants onboard and process their first live payment within 24 hours* — but no persistent team owns it. PTS is run as a pilot on that one value stream, for two planning cycles, on the existing project budget.

**Step 1 — Receive priorities and confirm outcomes**
PTS confirms the **Merchant Onboarding value stream** will carry the outcome, names a value-stream owner, and restates it. Early executability read to OSM: achievable, but only if onboarding, risk and know-your-customer (KYC) checks, and integration stop being three departments handing work to each other.

**Step 2 — Activate the four POM Enablers**
- *Leadership:* the VP Product shifts the team's measure from "projects delivered" to the 24-hour activation outcome.
- *Practices & Behaviors:* the team adopts continuous discovery with merchants and ships against tested hypotheses.
- *Organizational Architecture:* onboarding and integration capability are pulled into one persistent team; KYC review, which other value streams also use, becomes a Shared team's capability.
- *Culture & Relationships:* a blameless post-incident practice replaces cross-department finger-pointing.

**Step 3 — Structure the persistent team**
A persistent Merchant Onboarding **Core** team is formed: a product manager as its accountable product role, engineering, design, and an integrations engineer. Its capabilities, recorded on the capability map:

| Capability | Owned or consumed | From |
|---|---|---|
| Merchant application and activation | Owned | Merchant Onboarding (Core) |
| Merchant integration | Owned | Merchant Onboarding (Core) |
| KYC review | Consumed, on agreed terms (decision within four business hours) | Risk & Compliance (**Shared**; also serves Merchant Risk Monitoring) |
| Payments API and sandbox | Consumed, self-service | Payments Platform (**Platform-Enabled**) |
| Identity-document verification | Consumed; partner-sourced | A verification partner, with Risk & Compliance accountable for it |

No value stage needs an ad-hoc handoff. The team owns the customer outcome (merchant reaches first live payment); the value-stream owner owns the value-stream outcome and decides trade-offs inside the stream within OSM's allocation.

**Step 4 — Run the continuous lifecycle**
*Discover:* merchant interviews reveal KYC document upload is the drop-off point. *Define/Develop:* the team builds auto-verification. *Deliver:* released incrementally via CVDM. *Launch:* external GTM to the merchant base **and** internal change comms training the support team and the Risk & Compliance team on the new flow. *Support:* runs continuously, feeding drop-off signals back into Discover.

**Step 5 — Feedback and learning loop**
Organizational AER tracked: did the team adopt the new operating model (it did), engage with continuous discovery (partially — discovery slipped under delivery pressure), sustain it (at risk). Executability returned to OSM's decision forum: the 24-hour outcome is achievable, but discovery capacity must be protected. The value-stream owner acted on the read, reserving a fixed share of the team's time for discovery each planning cycle. CVDM operational feedback flows in on activation performance. The Health Check showed *Funding follows value-stream outcomes* At Risk, as expected on a project budget; the case for funding the stream by its outcome went to OSM's next decision forum.

> **What it produced:** A persistent Core team replacing the project model, consuming KYC from a Shared team and payments from a Platform-Enabled team without handoffs; all four POM Enablers active; a continuous lifecycle with a real two-sided Launch; and an Organizational AER signal that surfaced a discovery-capacity risk before it became an outcome miss. At the end of the pilot the recommended checks were met — the team stayed together, its capabilities were on the map, the discovery-capacity read was acted on, and an executability read had reached OSM — and the company chose to widen to Merchant Risk Monitoring next.

### Example 2 — Hospital network standing up a value-stream care team (Healthcare translation)

> **Scenario:** A hospital network's strategy (set via OSM) prioritizes *reducing avoidable cardiac readmissions*. Today, cardiology, pharmacy, discharge planning, and community follow-up operate as separate functions handing the patient between them. PTS is run as a pilot on the cardiac pathway to stand up a persistent cross-functional care operating model. *(Translation: "product team" → "care team"; "customer" → "patient"; "product lifecycle" → "care delivery lifecycle.")*

**Step 1 — Receive priorities and confirm outcomes**
The **Cardiac Care Pathway value stream** is confirmed to carry the outcome — *cardiac patients recover without avoidable 30-day readmission* — with a clinical value-stream owner. Executability read upward: achievable only if discharge and community follow-up stop being separate handoffs.

**Step 2 — Activate the four POM Enablers**
- *Leadership:* clinical leadership measures the team on the readmission outcome, not departmental throughput.
- *Practices & Behaviors:* the team adopts continuous learning from patient outcomes and treats each care protocol as a testable hypothesis.
- *Organizational Architecture:* cardiology, discharge, and community follow-up roles form one persistent care team around the pathway; pharmacy, which serves every pathway, stays a Shared team.
- *Culture & Relationships:* psychological safety lets nurses flag discharge gaps without blame.

**Step 3 — Structure the persistent team**
A persistent Cardiac Care Pathway **Core** team, with a clinical nurse lead as its accountable role, owns the patient's pathway end-to-end — no handoff to a separate discharge unit. Its capabilities:

| Capability | Owned or consumed | From |
|---|---|---|
| Cardiac discharge planning and teach-back | Owned | Cardiac Care Pathway (Core) |
| 72-hour community check-in | Owned | Cardiac Care Pathway (Core) |
| Medication reconciliation | Consumed, on agreed terms (before every discharge) | Pharmacy (**Shared**; serves every care pathway) |
| Patient record and remote monitoring | Consumed, self-service | Clinical Systems (**Platform-Enabled**) |
| Home nursing visits | Consumed; partner-sourced | A community nursing provider, with Community Care (**Shared**; would own home nursing in-house, for every pathway) accountable for it |

**Step 4 — Run the continuous lifecycle**
*Discover:* outcome data shows readmissions cluster where patients misunderstand medication. *Define/Develop:* a teach-back discharge protocol and a 72-hour community check-in. *Deliver:* embedded into the care pathway via CVDM. *Launch:* external — patients and carers briefed on the new follow-up; internal — discharge nurses and community teams trained and rostered to sustain it. *Support:* continuous follow-up, feeding readmission signals back to Discover.

**Step 5 — Feedback and learning loop**
Organizational AER: did the care team adopt the pathway model, engage with continuous outcome learning, and sustain the follow-up rota? The network reads this alongside CVDM's patient-side signal. Executability returned to OSM with the staffing reality of sustaining community follow-up. Funding stayed departmental during the pilot, so *Funding follows value-stream outcomes* read At Risk, and went to OSM with the pilot's results.

> **What it produced:** A persistent Core care team replacing four handoff-linked functions, consuming pharmacy from a Shared team and records from a Platform-Enabled team; a care delivery lifecycle with a genuine internal-change Launch; and an organizational-readiness signal feeding strategic reassessment of community-care staffing.

## Measurement

### 3E Application

PTS *is* the operating model, so 3E measures whether the operating model works. **Effectiveness is PTS's proof** — whether the operating model produces its value-stream outcomes. **Experience is its leading signal** — whether teams find the operating model empowering and coherent enough to adopt and sustain. **Efficiency is the table stake** — whether work flows through the operating model without waste, queues, or handoffs.

| 3E Dimension | What It Measures Here | Primary Signal |
|---|---|---|
| **Efficiency — Use** | Does work flow through the operating model without friction — minimal handoffs, queues, blockers, or rework across the lifecycle phases? | Leading |
| **Experience — Feel** | Do teams experience the operating model as empowering, clear, and coherent — are the POM Enablers felt, not just declared? | Leading |
| **Effectiveness — Achieve** | Does the operating model produce its value-stream outcomes — is strategy actually being operationalized? | Lagging |

**Primary 3E signal for this framework:** Effectiveness — PTS exists to operationalize strategy into delivered value-stream outcomes. But watch Experience as the leading signal: an operating model teams do not find empowering will not be adopted or sustained, and Organizational AER will fall before the outcome data confirms it.

### 8 Values Generated

This framework most directly drives:

| Value | How This Framework Generates It |
|---|---|
| **People Value** | The four POM Enablers — Leadership, Practices & Behaviors, Organizational Architecture, Culture & Relationships — build the empowered human system that delivers; PTS is the primary source of People Value |
| **Innovation Value** | Continuous discovery, hypothesis-driven delivery, and the full Discover-through-Support lifecycle create the conditions for innovation to compound |
| **Operational Value** | A value-stream operating model with minimized handoffs is how operational capability and delivery quality are built and sustained |

All 8 Values are touched across the full IIVCS — the table above shows which Values PTS is the primary generator of. People and Innovation Value are the operating model's most direct outputs; Operational Value is built through the flow it creates.

### AER Connection

PTS is the home of **Organizational AER** — the organizational half of the IIVCS dual-track behavioral measurement (CVDM holds the customer half). The 3E output drives each AER stage on the organizational track: **Efficiency → Adoption** (teams adopt an operating model they can work in without unnecessary friction, effort, or handoffs), **Experience → Engagement** (teams engage with a model they find empowering), **Effectiveness → Retention** (teams sustain a model that produces outcomes). PTS returns this Organizational AER — with People and Operational Value — upward to OSM's Governance & Measurement, where it converges with CVDM's Customer AER to validate whether strategic priorities are generating organizational value. The two tracks run in parallel because either alone produces nothing: an operating model teams adopt but that yields no customer outcome creates no value, and customer delivery no sustained operating model can support cannot last.

## System Health Check

These signals confirm PTS is working — not just being followed. Review them at each operating-model review.

| Signal | Healthy | At Risk | Remedy when At Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Team persistence | Teams form around value streams and stay; ownership and learning compound | Teams are assembled and disbanded per project; ownership resets each time | Step 3: restore the team's mandate and staffing so it stays together across work, not per project |
| End-to-end ownership | Teams own or consume the capabilities to deliver without ad-hoc handoffs | Delivering an outcome still requires handing off to another team | Step 3: change the team topology or the terms on which teams consume capabilities; a missing capability goes back to OSM to decide |
| Accountable product role | Each Core team has one named role accountable for its product | A Core team has no such role, or several | Step 3: name one accountable role per Core team |
| All four POM Enablers active | Leadership, Practices, Architecture, and Culture are all operating | Only structure changed; leadership still sets feature lists or culture rewards heroics | Step 2: re-activate the missing enabler |
| Lifecycle continuity | Discover and Support run continuously; phases overlap | Discover happens once at the start; Support is a separate downstream team | Step 4: bring Discover and Support back into the team as continuous work |
| Funding follows value-stream outcomes | Funding is allocated to persistent value-stream teams by value-stream outcome | Work is still funded by project (expected during a pilot) | OSM's decision forum (Step 6), with finance: move the stream's funding to its value-stream outcome |
| Organizational AER read | Team adoption, engagement, and retention of the model are measured and acted on | Only output/velocity is tracked; no read on whether the model is retained | Step 5: name who reads it and which decision it informs |

**When a signal is At Risk:** apply its remedy before the next operating-model review. A signal still At Risk at two reviews running goes to the executive sponsor. Where it means a value stream cannot carry its outcome, it also goes to OSM as an executability signal.

## Translation Notes

### Non-product contexts

PTS applies to any organization that must stand up an operating model to deliver outcomes through value streams. The following table maps default IIVCS language to equivalent terms by sector:

| IIVCS Default | Healthcare | Government | Professional Services | Manufacturing | Financial Services |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Customer | Patient / member | Citizen / resident | Client | End customer / buyer | Client / policyholder |
| Product team | Care team | Service delivery team | Engagement team | Production / value-chain team | Servicing team |
| Product lifecycle | Care delivery lifecycle | Service delivery lifecycle | Engagement lifecycle | Production lifecycle | Servicing lifecycle |
| POM Enablers | Clinical operating-model enablers | Service operating-model enablers | Delivery operating-model enablers | Production operating-model enablers | Servicing operating-model enablers |
| Launch | New protocol rollout + staff change | Service go-live + staff readiness | Solution rollout + team enablement | Production ramp + workforce change | Product go-live + ops readiness |

> **Core principle:** The logic is unchanged across sectors — activate the operating-model enablers, build persistent cross-functional teams around value streams, run a continuous lifecycle, and measure whether the model is adopted and sustained. Only the vocabulary shifts.

## Quick Reference

**Steps**

1. Receive governed priorities and confirm value-stream outcomes — *What must this operating model deliver, and can we carry it?*
2. Stand up the POM — activate the four Enablers — *What model will empower teams to deliver?*
3. Structure persistent cross-functional teams around value streams — *Who owns each value stream end-to-end?*
4. Run the continuous product lifecycle — *How do teams continuously turn priorities into adopted value?*
5. Activate the feedback and learning loop — *Is the model being adopted, sustained, and delivering outcomes?*

**Health signals at a glance**

- Teams are persistent and value-stream-aligned — not assembled per project
- Teams own delivery end-to-end without handoffs
- Each Core team has one named accountable product role
- All four POM Enablers are active — not just a reorg
- Discover and Support run continuously; phases overlap
- Funding follows value-stream outcomes, not projects
- Organizational AER (adoption, engagement, retention) is measured and acted on

**Product lifecycle:** Discover → Define → Develop → Deliver → Launch → Support — continuous, overlapping, with Launch covering both external GTM and internal change adoption.

**Primary 3E signal:** Effectiveness — PTS is proven by value-stream outcomes delivered; watch Experience (do teams find the model empowering?) as the leading early-warning.

**Primary 8 Values driven:** People Value (POM Enablers), Innovation Value (continuous discovery + full lifecycle), Operational Value (value-stream flow).
