# IIVCS Quick Self-Assessment

**A directional reading, in a few minutes, of how well strategy, teams, delivery and measurement work together in your organization. Eight questions; it is built on the [Insheights Integrated Value Creation System (IIVCS)](README.md).**

---

This is the fast self-orientation tool for the [Adoption Sequencing & Maturity Model](adoption-maturity.md). For a rigorous, scored evaluation that produces a full maturity profile, use the IIVCS Detailed Assessment.

For each question, pick the **highest statement that is *fully* true** today. **a = Early, b = Developing, c = Mature.** Each statement includes everything in the one before it. If a statement is only partly true, you have not reached that level: pick the one below. (This mirrors the gated scoring in the Detailed Assessment: you earn a level by clearing it, not by getting close.)

> **Answer for one value stream.** A **value stream** is the end-to-end chain of work through which you deliver one outcome to the people it serves: usually customers, sometimes your own staff. It starts when they ask for or need the outcome, and ends when they have it, for example getting a new customer up and running, or settling a claim. Strategy, teams and delivery connect on a value stream, so answer for one named value stream, not for the whole organization at once.

> **Who answers it.** A single leader can run this quick check for a directional read — but having a few people (strategy, operating model, delivery, measurement) **answer independently and then compare** gives a truer answer: each role sees only its own layer, and where their answers diverge is itself a finding (see [When several people answer](#when-several-people-answer)). For the rigorous version of this — independent scoring, evidence, and gated levels with defined roles — use the Detailed Assessment.

## The Questions

**1. Strategy and priorities.** How are priorities set and steered?
- **a)** Strategy is a slide deck; priorities are a list of projects.
- **b)** Priorities are results to achieve, some tied to value streams; reviewed regularly but rarely changed.
- **c)** Priorities rest on a clear diagnosis, clear choices (where to compete and where not, how to win) and written hypotheses. They are funded in rank order and steered by value stream. Strategy reviews read customer, staff and business results together, test the hypotheses, and change strategy when the evidence says so.

**2. How teams are organized.** How are delivery teams set up?
- **a)** Project teams, formed by department and disbanded at the end, handing work off.
- **b)** Some long-lived, mixed-skill teams on key value streams that deliver without routine handoffs, with partial support.
- **c)** Long-lived, mixed-skill teams on each value stream, with full support (leadership, ways of working, structure and culture). Discovery, building and support run side by side, not one after another. You measure whether staff take up, use and keep using these ways of working.

**3. Delivery and the customer.** How is delivery organized around what customers go through?
- **a)** Around internal processes, not the customer's journey.
- **b)** Key customer journeys are mapped to value streams; some stages of the work are defined; a few feedback routines run.
- **c)** Every journey is mapped to its value stream; each stage is defined by the result it gives the customer; feedback runs at every level, from how customers felt to whether priorities still hold; you measure whether customers take up, use and keep using what you deliver.

**4. Measuring delivery.** How do you measure whether value is delivered?
- **a)** You count activity and output, and each area measures differently.
- **b)** In places, you measure ease of use, how it feels, and the result achieved.
- **c)** You measure ease, feel and result everywhere, the same way, and read them in that order to find where a problem starts.

**5. What people do with what you deliver.** Do you measure whether people take it up, use it and keep using it?
- **a)** No, or only through satisfaction surveys.
- **b)** For one group, usually customers, on some journeys.
- **c)** For customers and for your own staff, side by side, reviewed together in strategy reviews.

**6. The value you create.** How do you measure the value your organization creates?
- **a)** Financial and operational results only, each function on its own.
- **b)** Several kinds of value; some traced back to the work behind them.
- **c)** All eight kinds (customer, financial, people, operational, market, strategic, innovation, impact), each built up from the work behind it, feeding strategy reviews.

**7. What happens to what you learn.** What happens to what you learn from customers and delivery?
- **a)** It stays with the team.
- **b)** It flows regularly to delivery leaders, each feedback routine has a named owner, and it has changed how delivery is organized, but it stops there.
- **c)** It travels from the customer-facing work, through the people who run delivery, to the people who set strategy; together with the business's results, it leads strategy to be re-examined.

**8. How people work.** What best describes how people work?
- **a)** Success means delivering the planned output; assumptions go untested.
- **b)** People talk about results, not outputs; in places, teams find out what customers need before building.
- **c)** Five habits are the norm: customer first; results over output; always learning what customers need; ideas tested as bets; shared ownership across functions.

## Read Your Result

1. **Your system level comes from the chain.** Questions 1, 2, 3 and 7 are the chain strategy → operating model → delivery → feedback, which the IIVCS calls OSM → PTS → CVDM → the Feedback System (see [How the questions map](#how-the-questions-map)). Your lowest answer among them is your level: **a** → *Early*, **b** → *Developing*, **c** → *Mature*, as in the [Maturity Model](adoption-maturity.md#maturity-across-the-suite). It is not an average: high answers elsewhere do not raise it.
2. **Read questions 4, 5, 6 and 8 as enablers.** They cover the measurement and the working culture the frameworks depend on. An enabler answered below your level is a finding: it limits how far the frameworks it supports can climb.
3. **Keep your eight answers as your profile**: the four chain answers, then the four enablers (for example: chain b a b a; enablers b b a b). Mixed answers are the most common result, and the most useful: the spread shows which connections are missing.
4. **Find your break and your starting point.** Your break is your lowest chain answer: the point where value leaks out of the system, and your adoption priority. It points to the part of the IIVCS to start with. The Maturity Model's [From an assessment result](adoption-maturity.md#from-an-assessment-result) gives the tie rule, the entry point the break points to, and what to do when there is no break.

### When several people answer

1. **Answer privately first.** Each person answers all eight questions alone, for the same value stream, before anyone compares.
2. **Line the answers up**, question by question.
3. **Treat every difference as a finding**, and note which roles gave which answer. People who run the stream day to day and people who govern it often see it differently, and that gap is worth knowing.
4. **Take the lower answer**, unless someone shows the group something it can all check, such as a document, a dashboard or a decision on record. Agreement reached by discussion alone does not count.
5. **Read your result** as above, from the answers that stand.

> **Want the full picture?** This quick check is directional only — a one-question read per element, no evidence test. The IIVCS Detailed Assessment applies evidence-anchored, gated criteria across all eight components and produces a maturity profile with strengths, constraint, and adoption priority. Use this to orient; use the detailed assessment to decide.

> **What a level means.** Maturity here is **adoption fidelity** — the frameworks present and connected — not proof of value created. A Mature reading means the machinery is in place and wired together, not that the organization is yet creating more value.

### How the questions map

Each question states, in plain words, one row of the [Maturity Model](adoption-maturity.md#maturity-across-the-suite): answers **a**, **b** and **c** hold that row's Early, Developing and Mature bars.

| Question | Maturity Model row | Role in the result |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Strategy and priorities | [Organization Strategy Model (OSM)](README.md#osm--organization-strategy-model) — Strategy & Governance | Chain |
| 2. How teams are organized | [Product Transformation System (PTS)](README.md#pts--product-transformation-system) — Operating Model (full support means the four [Product Operating Model (POM)](glossary.md#core-system-and-frameworks) Enablers) | Chain |
| 3. Delivery and the customer | [Customer Value Delivery Model (CVDM)](README.md#cvdm--customer-value-delivery-model) — Delivery & Realization (its feedback is the [five feedback loops](feedback.md)) | Chain |
| 4. Measuring delivery | [Efficiency, Experience, Effectiveness (3E)](3e.md) — Operational Measurement | Enabler |
| 5. What people do with what you deliver | [Adoption, Engagement, Retention (AER)](aer.md) — Behavioral Measurement | Enabler |
| 6. The value you create | [8 Values](8-values.md) — Organizational Outcomes | Enabler |
| 7. What happens to what you learn | [Feedback System](feedback.md) — Upstream Loops | Chain |
| 8. How people work | Product Mindset — Foundation | Enabler |
