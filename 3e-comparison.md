# Insheights 3E Operational Values Measurement Model — Comparison with Industry Standards

**ISO 9241-11 | Balanced Scorecard | Google HEART | NPS / CSAT / CES | AARRR**

A comparison of the Insheights 3E Operational Values Measurement Model against established usability, enterprise measurement, product analytics, and customer experience frameworks.

---

## Positioning

3E (Efficiency | Experience | Effectiveness) measures value delivery in sequence — from whether value can be accessed (Use), through whether it is felt positively (Feel), to whether it achieves intended outcomes (Achieve). It is applied consistently across every architecture element in the [Insheights Integrated Value Creation System (IIVCS)](README.md): Value Streams, Value Stages, Capabilities, Processes, and Products/Services.

The frameworks below each capture a portion of what 3E measures. Among them, 3E is designed to read its dimensions in a stated order, which is a reasoned hypothesis, and to apply one measurement language from process level to value stream level, mapped to an external taxonomy of customer value.

## Comparison by Framework

### ISO 9241-11 (Usability: Effectiveness, Efficiency, Satisfaction)

ISO 9241-11 is the international ergonomics standard for usability. Its 2018 edition defines usability for "a system, product or service" through three dimensions: Effectiveness, Efficiency and Satisfaction. It defines Satisfaction through the user's physical, cognitive and emotional responses, as they meet the user's needs and expectations.

**Alignment:** 3E borrows two ISO terms, Efficiency and Effectiveness, puts Experience where ISO has Satisfaction, and applies them beyond the use of a system, product or service, which is ISO's scope, to any element of the delivery system.

**What it does well:** Precise, internationally agreed definitions of usability, which make results comparable across studies and products. Usability work operationalizes them with task completion rates, error frequencies, and time on task.

**Key difference:** ISO 9241-11 is applied to the use of a system, product or service — whether specified users can achieve specified goals with it. ISO treats its three dimensions as parallel lenses applied together. 3E is applied across the value delivery architecture, from individual Process level to Value Stream level, and reads its dimensions in a stated order: Efficiency is the table stake, Experience is the bridge, and Effectiveness is the proof. 3E is designed to add an order of reading (Use → Feel → Achieve) and links to AER and the 8 Values; the order and the links are reasoned hypotheses, not yet validated.

### Balanced Scorecard (Kaplan & Norton)

The Balanced Scorecard organizes measurement into four perspectives — Financial, Customer, Internal Process, and Learning & Growth — to give leaders a balanced view of organizational performance beyond financial metrics alone.

**Alignment:** Partial structural mapping exists. The Customer perspective captures elements of Experience and Effectiveness — how customers perceive the organization and whether they are satisfied. The Internal Process perspective captures elements of Efficiency — how well operations run. Learning & Growth relates to People Value and Organizational AER rather than to 3E.

**What it does well:** It gives leaders a balanced set of perspectives beyond financial results, and its strategy maps make the assumed cause-and-effect between them explicit.

**Key difference:** The Balanced Scorecard's four perspectives are watched together. Its strategy maps assert causal links between perspectives, but they set no order in which to look when an outcome underperforms. 3E reads its three dimensions in a stated order: when Effectiveness underperforms, the practitioner checks Experience first, then Efficiency. The Balanced Scorecard is designed for strategic management and is commonly cascaded to business units and teams. Its customer perspective rests on a customer value proposition defined per strategy. 3E applies the same three questions at every architecture level, and maps to one external taxonomy, the Elements of Value, through Insheights' own mapping, which has not been validated. 3E is designed to add that order and a fixed leading/lagging classification at every element; both are reasoned hypotheses, not yet validated.

### Google HEART Framework

HEART (Happiness, Engagement, Adoption, Retention, Task Success) is a set of user-centered metric categories for web products and features, published by Google researchers in 2010. It is described on the [AER comparison](aer-comparison.md#google-heart), where three of its five categories are compared.

**Alignment:** Two of HEART's categories sit in 3E. HEART's authors say that Task Success "incorporates both effectiveness and efficiency" and that Happiness "incorporates satisfaction". So Task Success maps to both Efficiency (errors, time on task) and Effectiveness (completion), and Happiness maps to Experience. HEART's Adoption, Engagement and Retention are behaviors, and in IIVCS they belong to AER.

**What it does well:** It gives a product team a short list of categories to choose from, and a step-by-step way from a goal to a metric.

**Key difference:** HEART presents its categories to choose from, with no order between them, for one product or feature. 3E reads its three dimensions as a sequence (Efficiency, Experience, Effectiveness) that says where to look first when a result falls short, and applies the same three questions to any element of the delivery system, from value stream to process. 3E is designed to add that sequence and that reach; the sequence is a reasoned hypothesis, not yet validated.

---

### NPS / CSAT / CES

Net Promoter Score (NPS), Customer Satisfaction Score (CSAT), and Customer Effort Score (CES) are three widely used single-dimension customer measurement instruments.

- **CES** measures the effort a customer reports in accomplishing a task or resolving a request. In IIVCS it is an Experience reading of perceived effort; Efficiency reads observed friction.
- **CSAT** measures satisfaction with a specific interaction or experience. It is a point-in-time Experience signal.
- **NPS** measures stated advocacy likelihood — "How likely are you to recommend?" It is attitudinal: a stated intent, not the referral itself.

**Alignment:** Each instrument maps to one dimension of 3E. CES maps to Experience — it reports how much effort the customer felt, which Efficiency reads alongside observed friction. CSAT maps to Experience — it captures the feeling dimension at a specific interaction. NPS maps to Experience — it measures stated intent to recommend; the referral itself is a behavioral signal, recorded in AER (Recommendation).

**What it does well:** Each is short, cheap to run and comparable over time, and good practice adds the reason behind the score.

**Key difference:** Each instrument captures one valid reading. A score alone does not locate the cause; good practice adds the reason behind it. 3E gives each instrument a place, with CSAT and CES as Experience readings (CES reads perceived effort) and NPS as stated intent, and proposes an order in which to look for the cause: whether a declining score reflects friction preventing use (Efficiency), a poor feeling undermining confidence (Experience), or outcomes not materializing (Effectiveness). 3E is designed to add that place and that order; the order is a reasoned hypothesis, not yet validated.

---

### AARRR (Pirate Metrics)

AARRR (Acquisition → Activation → Retention → Referral → Revenue) is a growth framework for digital products and businesses, sequencing the stages a customer moves through from first awareness to revenue generation.

**Alignment:** Among the frameworks here, AARRR is the closest analogue to 3E's sequencing principle. Its stages are behaviors and revenue: Retention and Referral correspond to Customer AER's Retention and Recommendation, and Revenue to Financial Value in the 8 Values. 3E reads beneath them: Activation depends on Efficiency — the customer can access and use the product without friction.

**What it does well:** A simple, shared sequence for a growth team, with one metric per stage.

**Key difference:** AARRR is a company-centric growth funnel — it sequences the stages from the organization's perspective (acquire customers, get them activated, retain them, generate revenue). 3E is customer-outcome-centric — it sequences the customer's value realization (access value, feel it, achieve through it). AARRR's stages do not include how the customer felt between Activation and Retention; that is what 3E names as the bridge. 3E's hypothesis is that customers who are activated but do not feel value positively are less likely to persist to Retention. AARRR also applies to the acquisition and growth funnel, not to the capabilities and processes behind it. 3E is designed to add an Experience reading between Activation and Retention, and the same three questions across the delivery architecture; the link to outcomes is a reasoned hypothesis, not yet validated.

## The Distinctive Contribution of 3E

### Sequencing as a Diagnostic Tool

Among the frameworks compared here, ISO 9241-11, the Balanced Scorecard and HEART present their dimensions as parallel lenses or as categories to choose from, and AARRR orders the stages of a growth funnel. 3E's stated order — Efficiency is the table stake, Experience is the bridge, Effectiveness is the proof — is meant as the instruction for where to look when something is wrong: an Effectiveness gap is checked against Experience before concluding, and an Experience gap against Efficiency before acting. The order is a reasoned hypothesis, not yet validated ([3E — Limitations](3e.md#limitations)). If it holds, it would make root-cause analysis less dependent on the individual practitioner.

### Leading and Lagging Signal Classification

3E explicitly classifies its dimensions by signal timing: Efficiency and Experience are leading (operational and attitudinal signals that surface before outcomes materialize), and Effectiveness is lagging (outcome validation that surfaces last). Teams monitoring leading signals are expected to identify value failures before they register as outcome degradation. By the time a Retention drop or a revenue shortfall appears, the upstream failure is expected to have been present for some time. 3E's leading signal structure is designed to surface intervention opportunities before lagging indicators confirm the damage.

### Broad Coverage via Elements of Value

3E draws on Bain & Company's Elements of Value research — 30 B2C elements and 40 B2B elements — and Insheights maps each of the 70 to one of the three 3E dimensions. The mapping is Insheights' own, not Bain's, and has not been validated. Each dimension covers a defined set of customer value drivers, and together they provide broad coverage of what customers and organizations value. Among the frameworks compared here, 3E is distinctive in mapping its dimensions to an external, researched taxonomy of customer value rather than relying only on author-defined categories.

### Cross-Architectural Consistency

3E applies the same three questions — Efficiency, Experience, Effectiveness — from Value Stream level down to Process level, giving every element in the IIVCS a shared measurement language. A portfolio decision comparing two Value Streams uses the same language as a team improving a single Process. This consistency is designed to make cross-level comparison and root-cause tracing possible; frameworks built for a single level (HEART for products, AARRR for growth funnels, ISO 9241-11 for the use of a system, product or service) are not designed for that.

### Connection to Behavioral and Organizational Outcomes

3E does not terminate at measurement — it connects forward to AER (behavioral response) and on to the 8 Values (organizational outcomes). Each 3E dimension is expected to lead the corresponding AER stage (Efficiency enables Adoption, Experience sustains Engagement, Effectiveness earns Retention), and AER is expected to lead the 8 Values — Customer, Financial, People, Operational, and the compounding Values over time. These links are the model's reasoned hypotheses, not validated. Among the frameworks compared here, 3E is the one designed as a node in a connected outcome architecture.

## Limitations

This comparison is a structured argument, not an independent evaluation. 3E is Conceptual. Insheights chose the dimensions it is compared on and rated every framework, its own included. 3E has no validated benchmarks; its sequence and its links to AER and the 8 Values are reasoned hypotheses. The model's own limitations are in [3E — Limitations](3e.md#limitations).
