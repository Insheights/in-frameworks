# Insheights 3E Operational Values Measurement Model

**Efficiency | Experience | Effectiveness**

The measurement construct of the Insheights Integrated Value Creation System, developed by Insheights LLC.

The Insheights 3E Operational Values Measurement Model is how the Insheights Integrated Value Creation System (IIVCS) measures value — from the moment it is used, through how it feels, to what it ultimately achieves. Mapped to both B2B and B2C Elements of Value, 3E is the first layer of the IIVCS measurement language, 3E → [Adoption, Engagement, Retention (AER)](aer.md) → [8 Values](8-values.md). It gives [Organization Strategy Model (OSM)](README.md#osm--organization-strategy-model), [Product Transformation System (PTS)](README.md#pts--product-transformation-system), and [Customer Value Delivery Model (CVDM)](README.md#cvdm--customer-value-delivery-model) one set of questions at every architecture element: Value Stages, Capabilities, Processes, Products/Services, and Value Streams. Its signals are surfaced through CVDM's five feedback loops.

- **Efficiency** — measuring whether value is accessible without unnecessary friction, effort, cost, or complexity
- **Experience** — measuring whether value is being felt positively by customers and stakeholders
- **Effectiveness** — measuring whether value is delivering intended outcomes for the customer and the organization

**Product Mindset** is the operating philosophy of the IIVCS — and 3E is its measurement expression, translating the system's value promise into customer-grounded signals that outcomes can confirm.

How to run it: [3E Practitioners Guide](guides/3e-practitioners-guide.md). Signals, their home layers and reference bars: [Metrics and Indicators](metrics-and-indicators.md).

## The Three Dimensions

**Efficiency — Use**

The degree to which value is delivered without unnecessary friction, effort, cost, or complexity. Efficiency is the table stake of the 3E model: without it, Experience and Effectiveness are not expected to be reached. A customer who cannot use value without friction is not expected to persist long enough to feel it or achieve through it. If Experience and Effectiveness hold up over three consecutive readings while Efficiency stays weak, revisit the link.

Efficiency covers the value elements about using value: time, effort, cost, hassle, simplicity, transparency, and how things are organized, integrated and connected, together with Bain's B2B table stakes. It is primarily a leading, operational signal.

**Experience — Feel**

The degree to which customers and stakeholders feel the value being delivered — through trust, confidence, reduced anxiety, and positive engagement. Experience is the bridge between Use and Achieve. It is expected to be the earliest actionable signal of value realization, and to lead Effectiveness. If Experience improves and Effectiveness does not follow over three consecutive readings, revisit the link.

Experience covers the value elements about how value is felt: Bain's B2C emotional, life-changing and social-impact elements, and its B2B relationship and individual elements, availability, and two of its inspirational elements (hope, social responsibility). These are the elements expected to bear on whether customers persist, advocate, and return. Experience surfaces before outcomes can be confirmed, making it time-sensitive to measure. Its signals are attitudinal — what people report they feel or intend, such as CSAT, CES, NPS and, internally, eNPS. What people then do is measured in [AER](aer.md).

**Effectiveness — Achieve**

The degree to which intended outcomes are achieved — for the customer, the organization, and the value stream. Effectiveness is the proof of value creation. It validates that the system delivered what it promised, not merely that it operated.

Effectiveness covers the value elements about results: quality, risk, information, money and top line, performance (scalability, innovation), reach, flexibility, variety, configurability and sensory appeal, and Bain's B2B vision element — the elements that answer whether the value proposition was realized. It is primarily a lagging, outcome signal.

| Dimension | Sequence | Question It Answers | Value Elements Covered (Insheights' mapping) | Signal Type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Efficiency** | Use | Is value accessible without friction? | Use: time, effort, cost, simplicity, connection; B2B table stakes | Leading — operational (revisit after three contrary readings) |
| **Experience** | Feel | Is value being felt positively? | Feeling: emotional, life-changing, relationship and individual elements | Leading — attitudinal (some elements are slow; see below; revisit after three contrary readings) |
| **Effectiveness** | Achieve | Is value delivering intended outcomes? | Results: quality, risk, money, information, performance, reach, variety | Lagging — outcome |

## The Sequence — Use → Feel → Achieve

3E reads its dimensions in a stated order. The order is a reasoned hypothesis about how value is encountered, experienced, and validated; each link below says what would count against it.

**Use comes first.** A customer must be able to access and use value before they can feel it. Efficiency failures at this stage are expected to keep Experience and Effectiveness from being reached: if the system creates friction at the point of use, the customer is not expected to progress. If customers progress to Experience and Effectiveness over three consecutive readings while Efficiency fails, revisit the link.

**Feel comes second.** Once value is in use, the customer forms an experience — positive or negative. This feeling is expected to be the bridge to achievement: a customer who feels trust, confidence, and reduced anxiety is expected to persist toward outcomes, and a customer who feels friction, uncertainty, or disengagement is not, whether or not outcomes are available. If Effectiveness holds up over three consecutive readings while Experience stays weak, revisit the link.

**Achieve comes third.** Outcomes are validated after Use and Feel have been established. Effectiveness without Experience is expected to be fragile — customers who achieve outcomes but feel poor about the journey are expected to be more likely to churn. Effectiveness with strong Experience is expected to build loyalty and advocacy. If customers with weak Experience stay at the same rate as those with strong Experience over three consecutive readings, revisit the link.

```
Use  ──→  Feel  ──→  Achieve
 ↓          ↓           ↓
Efficiency  Experience  Effectiveness
(table      (bridge)    (proof)
 stake)
```

The sequence also sets the order in which feedback is expected to surface. Efficiency surfaces first, as friction in use. Experience is expected to be the earliest signal that value is being realized — it surfaces before outcomes can be confirmed, so it is expected to be the earliest realization signal teams can act on. Effectiveness confirms last.

## Mapping to the Elements of Value

3E draws on Bain & Company's Elements of Value research: 30 B2C elements (Almquist, Senior and Bloch, 2016) and 40 B2B elements (Almquist, Cleghorn and Sherer, 2018). Insheights maps each of the 70 to one of the three 3E dimensions. The mapping is Insheights' own; it is not made or validated by Bain. On this page, a *value element* is one of Bain's elements; an *architecture element* is a Value Stage, Capability, Process, Product/Service or Value Stream.

**Efficiency covers:**

| B2C Elements | B2B Elements |
|---|---|
| Saves time, Reduces effort, Simplifies, Avoids hassles, Reduces cost, Organizes, Integrates, Connects | Time savings, Reduced effort, Cost reduction, Decreased hassles, Transparency, Simplification, Organization, Connection, Integration, Acceptable price, Meeting specifications, Regulatory compliance, Ethical standards |

**Experience covers:**

| B2C Elements | B2B Elements |
|---|---|
| Reduces anxiety, Wellness, Rewards me, Nostalgia, Design/aesthetics, Badge value, Fun/entertainment, Attractiveness, Provides access, Therapeutic value, Motivation, Heirloom, Affiliation and belonging, Provides hope, Self-actualization, Self-transcendence | Reduced anxiety, Design & aesthetics, Growth & development, Fun & perks, Commitment, Stability, Cultural fit, Expertise, Responsiveness, Availability, Network expansion, Marketability, Reputational assurance, Hope, Social responsibility |

**Effectiveness covers:**

| B2C Elements | B2B Elements |
|---|---|
| Quality, Makes money, Informs, Variety, Sensory appeal, Reduces risk | Product quality, Scalability, Innovation, Risk reduction, Reach, Flexibility, Component quality, Configurability, Variety, Information, Improved top line, Vision |

The mapping shows broad coverage of what customers and organizations value. It does not show that each value element is being measured (see [Limitations](#limitations)). Some value elements Experience covers, such as self-actualization or heirloom value, are felt only over long periods: read them as slow signals, not leading ones.

## Application Across the IIVCS

3E applies across OSM, PTS, and CVDM. Each framework engages the dimensions it owns, reflecting the distinct role each plays in the value creation system.

**OSM** is read mainly on Effectiveness (are strategic outcomes being achieved?) and Experience (do stakeholders have confidence in the direction?). Its Efficiency reading is clarity: does strategic direction reach the teams that carry it without friction?

**PTS** treats Efficiency as a table stake — a transformation that is not efficient is not expected to take hold; if one takes hold over three consecutive readings while its Efficiency stays weak, revisit the link. It is ultimately measured on whether the product operating model delivers the right outcomes (Effectiveness) and whether teams and customers feel the transformation positively (Experience). Efficiency is the floor; Effectiveness and Experience determine whether the transformation succeeded.

**CVDM** enables 3E. CVDM's customer journey anchor, value stream delivery, and five feedback loops are the mechanism through which Efficiency, Experience, and Effectiveness are surfaced, measured, and returned upstream to refine the system.

Each Product Mindset principle has a direct role in 3E:

| Product Mindset Principle | How It Shows Up in 3E |
|---|---|
| Customer obsession | Experience is measured from the customer's perspective first — their journey, their feeling, their outcome |
| Outcome over output | Effectiveness measures value received, not activity completed — the test of whether the system delivered |
| Continuous discovery | Experience feedback is the earliest realization signal, read often — teams stay in contact with how value is being felt before outcomes can be confirmed |
| Hypothesis-driven thinking | Each dimension is a testable assumption — teams define expected Efficiency, Experience, and Effectiveness, then validate against reality |
| Cross-functional ownership | 3E spans the full value delivery chain — no single function owns Efficiency, Experience, or Effectiveness in isolation |

## 3E in Practice

3E applies consistently across architecture elements, enabling comparison and decision-making across OSM, PTS, and CVDM.

| Architecture Element | Efficiency | Experience | Effectiveness |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Value Stage** | Does it activate without friction, delay, or unnecessary effort? | Does the customer feel positive, confident, and supported at this stage? | Does it deliver its intended contribution to the customer outcome? |
| **Capability** | Does it activate without waste, friction, or unnecessary cost? | Do teams and customers feel it working positively? | Does it deliver its intended contribution to the value stage? |
| **Process** | Does it execute without friction, delay, or unnecessary effort? | Do participants feel it is fair, clear, and supportive? | Does it produce its intended outcome consistently? |
| **Product/Service** | Can customers access and use it without friction? | Do customers feel positive, confident, and supported? | Does it deliver the intended value proposition? |
| **Value Stream** | Does it activate and deliver without unnecessary cost or friction? | Do customers and teams experience it positively? | Does it achieve its strategic outcomes? |

This consistency gives OSM, PTS, and CVDM shared questions for portfolio decisions — which Capabilities to invest in, which Processes to improve, which Products/Services to continue or sunset, and which Value Streams to prioritize or rebalance.

## Limitations

3E is a structured measurement model, not a validated instrument. Applied honestly, it has real boundaries:

- **A reasoned model on a mapping, not a validated one.** The 3E dimensions are Insheights' own mapping of Bain's 70 Elements of Value. The mapping shows the dimensions cover what customers and organizations value; it does not mean each value element is being measured, and it does not generate signal. The Use → Feel → Achieve sequence is reasoned from it, not a statistically established causal chain. Treat it as a disciplined hypothesis about where value breaks down, then confirm it with evidence; do not treat the arrows as proven causality. Each link on this page is stated with what would count against it: if the next signal does not follow over three consecutive readings, revisit the link.
- **No external benchmarks.** Unlike NPS, CSAT, and CES, 3E has no published industry reference ranges. You can compare an architecture element against its own baseline over time, but not against peers, until benchmark ranges exist.
- **Instrumentation-dependent.** Applying 3E well requires at least one observable signal per dimension for each architecture element you read. Organizations without analytics or feedback infrastructure will struggle to source Efficiency (observable friction) and Effectiveness (outcome) signals, and the model degrades to opinion.
- **Experience is the hardest dimension to measure.** Capturing how value is *felt* — before outcomes can be confirmed — is the most demanding part of the model. Weak Experience instrumentation collapses 3E back to a lagging Effectiveness read, losing its leading-indicator value.

**When not to use it:** one-off interactions where a single instrument (CES or CSAT) is sufficient; contexts with no defined value promise or no access to signal; early discovery where there is not yet anything to measure.

## Summary

3E measures value the way customers experience it — in sequence, across dimensions, and grounded in what matters to them.

Efficiency asks whether value can be used. Experience asks whether it is felt. Effectiveness asks whether it is achieved. Together, they form the first layer of the IIVCS measurement language, 3E → AER → 8 Values — from strategic intent in OSM, through transformation in PTS, to delivery and feedback in CVDM.

The feedback runs upstream — Efficiency surfacing first as friction, Experience as the expected earliest sign that value is being realized, Effectiveness confirming last — and returns signal that refines where and how the system creates value.
