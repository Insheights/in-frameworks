# AER Behavioral Measurement Model — Comparison with Industry Standards

**Prosci ADKAR | Kotter 8-Step | McKinsey 7-S | Employee Engagement Surveys | Net Promoter Score | Google HEART**

A comparison of the Insheights AER Behavioral Measurement Model against established change management, organizational health, and customer measurement frameworks.

---

## Positioning

AER (Adoption | Engagement | Retention) measures behavioral response to value delivery across two parallel tracks — Customer AER and Organizational AER. It occupies the behavioral signal layer between [Efficiency, Experience, Effectiveness (3E)](3e.md) (how value is delivered) and the 8 Values (what the organization generates as a result).

Each of the frameworks below covers part of what AER reads. Among them, AER is designed to read both behavioral tracks, customer and organizational, in one model, inside a connected value delivery architecture (3E → AER → 8 Values). The Service-Profit Chain is the closest precedent for linking the two sides (see [Related Prior Work](iivcs-comparison.md#related-prior-work)); how AER differs from it is set out under [Dual-Track Structure](#dual-track-structure).

## Comparison by Framework

### Prosci ADKAR

ADKAR (Awareness → Desire → Knowledge → Ability → Reinforcement) is Prosci's individual change model. It diagnoses where a specific person is blocked in a change.

**Alignment:** ADKAR's Ability and Reinforcement describe one person reaching and sustaining a change; Organizational AER's Adoption and Retention read the same at team level. ADKAR's Desire is a person's decision to support and participate in the change; it is attitudinal and has no AER counterpart.

**What it does well:** It shows a change manager where to intervene with a specific person. Prosci measures a change's outcomes as speed of adoption, ultimate utilization and proficiency, and links them to the project's results.

**Key difference:** ADKAR and Prosci's measures are scoped to one change at a time. Organizational AER is designed to read one operating model at each operating-model review, and Customer AER reads the customer track at its own loops' cadences. Prosci's per-change measures run alongside Organizational AER and can inform practice coverage. AER is designed to add the customer track and, for Organizational AER, a read at each operating-model review, not per change; the link to outcomes is a reasoned hypothesis, not yet validated.

### Kotter's 8-Step Model

Kotter sequences the leadership actions required to execute large-scale change: create a sense of urgency, build a guiding coalition, form a strategic vision, enlist a volunteer army, enable action by removing barriers, generate short-term wins, sustain acceleration, and institute change.

**Alignment:** Kotter's last steps — generate short-term wins, sustain acceleration, institute change — ask the question Organizational AER Retention reads: did the change become the way we work?

**What it does well:** It sequences what leaders need to do to carry a large change through, from creating urgency to instituting the change.

**Key difference:** Kotter prescribes what leaders should do, and uses short-term wins to show progress. AER is designed to add a behavioral reading of whether those last steps produced lasting change. It runs alongside Kotter's short-term wins, which can inform it; the link to outcomes is a reasoned hypothesis, not yet validated.

---

### McKinsey 7-S

7-S maps whether seven organizational elements — Strategy, Structure, Systems, Staff, Skills, Style, and Shared Values — are mutually aligned. It is a systemic diagnostic used to identify misalignment that blocks execution.

**Alignment:** 7-S describes organizational conditions; AER reads behavioral response to those conditions. A 7-S misalignment in Systems or Style would be expected to surface as an Adoption or Engagement gap in Organizational AER.

**What it does well:** It gives leaders one shared set of seven elements to test against each other, so that a misalignment can be named.

**Key difference:** 7-S is structural and diagnostic; AER is behavioral and signal-based. 7-S helps explain why adoption might be failing; AER is designed to show that it is failing. Used together, AER surfaces the behavioral signal and 7-S helps identify the structural cause. AER is designed to add that behavioral reading, on both tracks; the link to outcomes is a reasoned hypothesis, not yet validated.

---

### Employee Engagement Surveys (Gallup Q12 and equivalents)

Employee engagement surveys measure how employees feel about their work, manager, team, and organization. The Gallup Q12 and similar instruments capture attitudinal indicators.

**Alignment:** Organizational AER Engagement is the behavioral counterpart to the attitudinal engagement survey. Both target organizational health and the quality of employee participation: the survey through sentiment, AER through behavior.

**What it does well:** It reads how people feel about their work across a whole organization, with the same questions each time, so the reading can be compared over time.

**Key difference:** Engagement surveys measure how people feel; AER reads what people do. A team can score well on a satisfaction survey and still fail to adopt new practices, or leave at elevated rates. In IIVCS, survey scores are an attitudinal 3E Experience signal. AER is designed to add behavioral team signals read from team records, and a customer track; the link to outcomes is a reasoned hypothesis, not yet validated.

---

### Net Promoter Score

NPS measures customers' stated likelihood to recommend, used as a proxy for customer loyalty.

**Alignment:** Customer AER is the behavioral counterpart to NPS. Where Customer AER's R is Recommendation, it points at the same advocacy NPS targets, but as observed behavior (customers who stayed, returned, and referred) rather than stated likelihood. NPS asks "would you?"; AER is designed to record "did you?"

**What it does well:** One simple question that can be compared over time; good practice also collects the reason behind the score. Bain's Net Promoter System builds a feedback practice around it (see [Related Prior Work](iivcs-comparison.md#net-promoter-system-bain--company)).

**Key difference:** NPS is a stated-likelihood metric from a survey; AER is designed to record what customers then do. Its employee variant, eNPS, is a separate survey; neither records behavior. In AER, R is Retention or, by sector, Recommendation ([AER](aer.md#the-two-tracks)). A customer can give a high score and still churn. In IIVCS, NPS is a 3E Experience signal, and the referral itself is recorded in Customer AER. AER is designed to add that behavioral record, on both tracks; the link to outcomes is a reasoned hypothesis, not yet validated.

### Google HEART

HEART (Happiness, Engagement, Adoption, Retention, Task Success) is a set of user-centered metric categories for web products and features, published by Google researchers in 2010. Teams choose the categories that fit a product's goals and work through Goals, Signals and Metrics for each.

**Alignment:** HEART uses the same three words as AER: Adoption, Engagement and Retention. In HEART they are behavioral measures of a product's users. Adoption counts how many new users start using a product in a period. Engagement is the frequency, intensity or depth of their use. Retention is how many users from one period are still present later. Customer AER reads the same three behaviors.

**What it does well:** It turns product goals into metrics a team can track, step by step from goal to signal to metric. It asks teams to decide explicitly which categories to include, since not every category fits every product. It combines attitudinal data (Happiness, from surveys) with behavioral data (from logs) at scale.

**Key difference:** HEART's categories are a menu for one product or feature, and it keeps attitudinal and behavioral categories side by side. AER reads only behavior, as three stages, on two tracks: Customer AER for the value offered, and Organizational AER for the people delivering it. It sits between 3E and the 8 Values. HEART's other two categories have their IIVCS home in 3E: Happiness in Experience, and Task Success in Efficiency and Effectiveness (see the [3E comparison](3e-comparison.md#google-heart-framework)). AER is designed to add a behavioral reading on both tracks of one operating model, linked to how value is delivered and to what it produces; the link to outcomes is a reasoned hypothesis, not yet validated.

## The Distinctive Contribution of AER

### Dual-Track Structure

AER reads the same three stages — Adoption, Engagement and Retention — on two parallel tracks against one operating model: Customer AER, whose home is CVDM, and Organizational AER, whose home is PTS and which is read from team records rather than surveys. Among the frameworks compared here, none reads both tracks in one model. The closest precedent, the Service-Profit Chain, links employee and customer outcomes to profit, but of the three stages it reads only Retention (as loyalty) on each side, not Adoption or Engagement (see [Related Prior Work](iivcs-comparison.md#related-prior-work)). The difference is one of design, not of evidence: neither the chain nor AER's paired reading has been validated.

The reason for two tracks is that organizational value is expected to depend on both. A delivery system that gains customer adoption while losing organizational capability is not expected to last. A delivery system with strong internal engagement that fails to produce customer retention is not creating value.

### Leading Indicator Architecture

AER occupies the middle layer of a three-layer measurement sequence: 3E signals surface first (operational), AER signals surface next (behavioral), 8 Values signals surface last (organizational impact). In this sequence, AER Adoption and Engagement are expected to lead value performance (a reasoned hypothesis): a decline in either is expected to be visible before it registers as Customer, Financial, or People Value degradation. Established frameworks measure stated intent (NPS) or lagging outcomes (financial metrics), or diagnose structural conditions (7-S) or individual progress through a change (ADKAR). AER is designed to act as an early warning within a connected architecture.

### Behavioral, Not Attitudinal

AER reads what customers and teams do, not how they feel. Satisfaction is a condition; behavior is the signal. This is the line between AER and attitudinal instruments such as NPS and engagement surveys, which belong to 3E Experience. Adoption rates, engagement depth, retention curves, and recommendation frequency are observable and trackable, and are expected to be more actionable than sentiment scores alone.

## Limitations

This comparison is a structured argument, not an independent evaluation. AER is Conceptual. Insheights chose the dimensions it is compared on and rated every framework, its own included. AER has no validated benchmarks, and the links it relies on (3E → AER → 8 Values, and the dual-track pairing) are reasoned hypotheses. The model's own limitations, including that Organizational AER is the harder track to instrument, are in [AER — Limitations](aer.md#limitations).
