# Insheights 8 Organizational Values Measurement Model — Comparison with Industry Standards

**Balanced Scorecard | IIRC 6 Capitals | EFQM Model | ESG / GRI Standards | [Objectives and Key Results (OKRs)](glossary.md#external-frameworks-referenced)**

A comparison of the Insheights 8 Organizational Values Measurement Model against established strategic performance, organizational excellence, integrated reporting, and goal-setting frameworks.

---

## Positioning

The Insheights 8 Values model measures organizational value generation — what the organization produces across eight interconnected dimensions as a result of its strategy, transformation, and delivery operating through the [Insheights Integrated Value Creation System (IIVCS)](README.md). It occupies the organizational outcome layer of the measurement architecture: downstream of [Efficiency, Experience, Effectiveness (3E)](3e.md) (how value is delivered) and [Adoption, Engagement, Retention (AER)](aer.md) (behavioral response to delivery), and upstream of governance and external reporting.

Each of the frameworks below captures part of what the 8 Values measures. Among them, the 8 Values is designed around the combination of three choices: tracing value generation to an operational delivery system ([Organization Strategy Model (OSM)](README.md#osm--organization-strategy-model)/[Product Transformation System (PTS)](README.md#pts--product-transformation-system)/[Customer Value Delivery Model (CVDM)](README.md#cvdm--customer-value-delivery-model)), modeling compounding relationships between dimensions rather than treating them independently, and placing value measurement as the lagging layer that 3E and AER lead, between delivery signals and governance reporting. The Balanced Scorecard's strategy maps are the closest precedent for the compounding idea (see below).

## Comparison by Framework

### Balanced Scorecard (Kaplan & Norton)

The Balanced Scorecard (BSC) measures organizational performance across four perspectives: Financial, Customer, Internal Processes, and Learning & Growth. It is used to translate strategy into operational metrics and track execution across dimensions that financial reporting alone cannot capture.

**Alignment:** The BSC's four perspectives map to 8 Values dimensions — Financial (Financial Value), Customer (Customer Value + Market Value), Internal Processes (Operational Value), Learning & Growth (People Value + Innovation Value). The BSC rests on the same premise the 8 Values makes: that organizational performance is multi-dimensional. Strategy maps — the BSC's mechanism for showing cause-and-effect between perspectives — are the closest analog to the 8 Values compounding model.

**What it does well:** It translates strategy into a balanced set of measures beyond financial results, and its strategy maps make the assumed cause-and-effect between perspectives explicit.

**Key difference:** The BSC measures performance *toward* a strategy; the 8 Values measures value *generated through* an operating system. In the BSC, the links between perspectives are drawn per strategy, in a strategy map. The 8 Values specifies its compounding relationships in the model itself, as reasoned hypotheses: Customer Value behavioral signals are expected to drive Financial Value; People Value health is expected to drive Operational Value capability stock; Market Value is expected to be earned through Customer Value and Strategic Value consistency, not set independently. The BSC asks "are we meeting targets in each dimension?" The 8 Values asks "which dimensions are generating, which are consuming, and where is the upstream cause?" The BSC pairs leading and lagging indicators, constructed per strategy map. The 8 Values sits within a three-layer sequence — 3E (leading), AER (bridge), 8 Values (organizational outcome) — that is designed to make leading indicators explicit and connected to delivery. The 8 Values is designed to add that connection to the delivery architecture and the behavioral signal layer beneath it; the link to outcomes is a reasoned hypothesis, not yet validated.

### IIRC 6 Capitals (International Integrated Reporting Council)

The Integrated Reporting (<IR>) Framework was issued by the IIRC and is now maintained by the IFRS Foundation. It defines six forms of capital that organizations depend on and affect: Financial, Manufactured, Intellectual, Human, Social & Relationship, and Natural. It is used by boards and investors to understand how organizations create, preserve, and erode value across all capital forms.

**Alignment:** The 8 Values model is designed to map to the six capitals for IR-compatible framing. The 8 Values measure the *flow* of value generation; the six capitals describe the *stock* of capital held. Every value dimension maps to one or more capitals: Customer Value and Market Value → Social & Relationship Capital; Financial Value → Financial Capital; People Value → Human Capital; Operational Value → Manufactured and Intellectual Capital; Strategic Value and Innovation Value → Intellectual Capital; Impact Value → Natural and Social Capital. The mapping is many-to-many and not an audited translation (see [8 Values — Limitations](8-values.md#limitations)).

**What it does well:** It gives boards and investors one view of the forms of capital an organization depends on and affects, and it aims to support integrated thinking and decision-making inside the organization as well as reporting.

**Key difference:** <IR> is a reporting framework for external stakeholders. It sets reporting principles; it does not trace how capitals are generated in operations. An IR report confirms that Human Capital increased; it does not explain whether that increase came from People Value improvements in the operating model, from an acquisition, or from a favourable market for talent. The 8 Values is designed to trace capital generation to its source in the IIVCS delivery architecture, and an IR report can be one external destination for what it reads internally. The 8 Values is designed to add that operational reading; the link to outcomes is a reasoned hypothesis, not yet validated.

### EFQM Model (European Foundation for Quality Management)

The EFQM Model, since its 2020 edition and in the 2025 edition, asks three questions (Direction, Execution and Results) across seven criteria, and assesses them with the RADAR logic (Results, Approaches, Deployment, Assessment and Refinement).

**Alignment:** Its Results question asks what the organization has achieved, which is the ground the 8 Values measures as organizational outcomes.

**What it does well:** One model and one assessment logic that an organization can use to examine its direction, its execution and its results together.

**Key difference:** EFQM is an assessment model; the 8 Values is designed for continuous internal reading within a delivery architecture. EFQM tells you where you are against its criteria at the time of an assessment; the 8 Values is designed to show how value is being generated and where it is compounding or leaking over time. In the 8 Values, Customer Value and People Value are expected to be connected (People Value → Operational Value → Customer Value delivery chain, a reasoned hypothesis). The 8 Values is designed to add a reading connected to the delivery architecture and to the behavioral signals beneath it; the link to outcomes is a reasoned hypothesis, not yet validated.

### ESG / GRI Standards (Environmental, Social, Governance)

ESG frameworks — such as the GRI (Global Reporting Initiative) Standards and the ISSB (International Sustainability Standards Board) standards — define how organizations measure and disclose their environmental, social, and governance performance. They are widely used for external accountability and disclosure of non-financial impact.

**Alignment:** Impact Value is the dimension of the 8 Values most directly addressed by ESG frameworks. The external layer of Impact Value — societal, environmental, and community contribution — maps to the Environmental and Social dimensions of ESG. The internal layer — mission advancement and governance integrity — maps to the Governance dimension. The 8 Values is designed to produce operational data on Impact Value that can inform ESG disclosures.

**What it does well:** Defined, comparable disclosure standards that external stakeholders, regulators and rating agencies can rely on.

**Key difference:** ESG frameworks are disclosure and accountability instruments — they define what organizations must report to external stakeholders, regulators, and rating agencies. ESG tells the market how the organization performed against sustainability metrics; it is not designed to tell the organization how its operational behavior is generating or eroding impact. Impact Value treats mission and societal contribution as an *operational dimension* that compounds internally: strong Impact Value is expected to attract People Value (people want to work for organizations with genuine purpose), amplify Market Value (stakeholders trust organizations that live their stated values), and deepen Customer Value (customers trust organizations with integrity). These links are reasoned hypotheses. The 8 Values has no equivalent of ESG's prescriptive disclosure standards, so organizations need both: the 8 Values for operational impact reading, ESG standards for external accountability. The 8 Values is designed to add that operational reading; the link to outcomes is a reasoned hypothesis, not yet validated.

### OKRs (Objectives & Key Results)

OKRs are a goal-alignment and progress-tracking system. Organizations set ambitious Objectives (qualitative direction) and measurable Key Results (quantitative proof), cascaded across the organization at company, team, and individual levels. OKRs are used to align execution across large organizations and track progress against strategic intent.

**Alignment:** OKRs are framework-agnostic — they can be written for any dimension of organizational performance. In practice, OKRs are often written for dimensions that correspond to 8 Values: revenue OKRs (Financial Value), customer acquisition OKRs (Customer Value), hiring and capability OKRs (People Value), product launch OKRs (Innovation Value). OKRs could function as the tracking layer within the 8 Values measurement system — setting objectives that correspond to specific value dimensions and measuring progress through key results that mirror AER and 3E signals.

**What it does well:** A light, widely understood way to set direction, track progress, and align teams through cascading.

**Key difference:** OKRs define what the organization is trying to achieve and whether it is making progress. They do not define what organizational value means, how it is generated, or how value dimensions compound. An OKR system tells a team whether they hit their target; the 8 Values is designed to show whether the organization is generating value and through which mechanisms. Objectives are set and tracked per team or unit, with alignment through cascading rather than through a modeled compounding system: a team can achieve all of its People Value OKRs while Operational Value is degrading upstream. The two are complementary: OKRs can be structured to carry 8 Values priorities, and the 8 Values profile can show which dimensions need OKR investment. The 8 Values is designed to add a definition of organizational value and the compounding links between its dimensions; the link to outcomes is a reasoned hypothesis, not yet validated.

**How they work together:** [Integration — OKRs](integration.md#okrs-objectives-and-key-results).

## The Distinctive Contribution of the 8 Values

### Operational Grounding

Every framework in this comparison measures outcomes or conditions — they observe what the organization has produced or what state it is in. The 8 Values is designed to measure value generated *through specific operational mechanisms*: OSM generates Strategic Value and sets Innovation Value direction; PTS generates People Value, Operational Value, and the Innovation pipeline; CVDM generates Customer Value and Market Value through delivery. Each value dimension is designed to be traced to an operational source. An organization using the 8 Values is meant to answer not just "how much Customer Value are we generating?" but "which value stage in which value stream is failing to deliver customer outcomes, and what does the 3E profile of that stage show?"

Among the frameworks compared here — the Balanced Scorecard, EFQM, <IR>, ESG and OKRs — each measures at the outcome layer. The 8 Values is designed to measure at the outcome layer and connect backward to the operational source.

### Compounding Model

Among the frameworks compared here, the 8 Values is the one designed to specify compounding relationships between value dimensions in the model itself. The BSC's strategy maps are the closest precedent: they draw cause-and-effect per strategy. The 8 Values specifies directional relationships, as reasoned hypotheses: People Value health is expected to drive Operational Value capability, which is expected to drive Customer Value delivery consistency, and in turn Financial Value sustainability and Market Value reputation. Impact Value is expected to deepen People Value and Market Value. Strategic Value direction is expected to determine where Innovation Value investment compounds.

The model is designed to give a gap in any dimension a diagnosis path: trace upstream in the compounding chain to find the root cause before acting on the downstream symptom.

### Leading Indicator Architecture

The 8 Values is the third layer of a three-layer measurement sequence: 3E signals surface first (operational delivery), AER signals surface next (behavioral response), 8 Values signals surface last (organizational value generation). This sequence makes the 8 Values a *lagging* measurement of what the *leading* signals point to. An Efficiency gap in a critical capability (3E) is expected to show as an Adoption decline in Organizational AER, and that as an Operational Value degradation in the 8 Values — before the organizational outcome registers. These links are the model's reasoned hypothesis, to be confirmed with evidence.

The Balanced Scorecard introduces leading indicators through strategy maps. The 8 Values takes its lead from 3E and AER, in a sequence designed to be read from delivery signals upward.

### Dual Reporting Direction

Among the frameworks compared here, the 8 Values is designed to read in two directions: upward to internal governance (OSM), and outward to external reporting through the six-capitals mapping, which is framing, not an audited translation.

## Limitations

This comparison is a structured argument, not an independent evaluation. The 8 Values model is Conceptual. Insheights chose the dimensions it is compared on and rated every framework, its own included. The 8 Values has no validated benchmarks; its compounding relationships and its lead from 3E and AER are reasoned hypotheses, and its mapping to the six capitals is framing, not an audited translation. The model's own limitations are in [8 Values — Limitations](8-values.md#limitations).
